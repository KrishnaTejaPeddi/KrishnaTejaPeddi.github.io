this was like a week ago but i learned about a Chinese historian called Sima Qian. I was interested on how we know so much about Chinese dynasties, China has a very interesting history in my opinion but i never understood how we had so many details about ancient China for like lets say the Shang dynasty or the Han dynasty, so I looked into it and it turns out that there's this great historian who went to great lengths to preserve a lot of China's history, Sima Qian, of course he is not the only historian to document China's history but he was certainly one of the more remarkable ones as he was very thorough and rigorous with his work. he'd cite his sources, traveled extensively through China, and even talked to multiple accounts that conflicted with each other, this might seem commonplace today but for his time this was very thorough. he was alive during the Han dynasty, and the main reason he was so focused on preserving China's history actually was because his father, Sima Tan, had started a huge project before he died, and he asked Sima Qian to finish it, making this book called "Shiji" wasn't just for history, but also a way for him to honour his dad's legacy. his book "Shiji" became extremely influential, the Shiji mixed genealogies, essays, biographies, and chronological records which became the standard format that later Chinese dynasty history books used for nearly 2000 years. the Shiji also did not limit itself to focusing on emperors and also discussed merchants, assassins, wandering knights, scholars, etc. Sima Qian was very dedicated to preserving history, not just the emperors. His work was formatted with Basic Annals, histories of emperors and major rulers, Chronological Tables, timelines to help keep track of when each event happened, Treatises which were essays on topics like economics, rituals, calendars and music, Hereditary Houses, the history of noble families and states, and biographies, stories of individual people. the biographies tend to be the part most people talk about as it's where most of the memorable characters appear. some of the most famous biographies of people in the Shiji are assassins, like a famous example is Jing Ke, an assassin who wanted to kill Qin Shi Huang (the leader who unified China when it was disjointed, buried with the Terracotta Army) at the time Qin Shi Huang was known as the King of Qin and was conquering its rival states one by one. one of these states was the state of Yan. Jing Ke was sent by Prince Dan of Yan on what was basically a suicide mission, the idea was simple, kill the King of Qin, Qin falls into chaos, this gives the state of Yan time to survive, and Jing Ke knew that he would likely die in this process. According to Sima Qian's account, the plan went like this: Jing Ke entered Qin's court pretending to give gifts, one gift was a map of Yan territory, inside this rolled up map was a dagger, when the map was opened Jing Ke grabbed the dagger and attacked. Unfortunately for him the King of Qin broke free and fled, after a chase around in the palace, the king drew his sword and fought Jing Ke, he won and Jing Ke died. While Jing Ke died Sima Qian admired the ambition. this assassination attempt happened around 80 years before Sima Qian was born, he learned about it through official state records, court documents, earlier written history texts and oral accounts. another famous biography is the biography of Confucius, Sima Qian wrote a major biography on Confucius which helped preserve what much of later generations knew of him, or for example Fan Li, a statesman who helped defeat a rival kingdom then retired and became a wealthy merchant, Sima Qian used him as an example of intelligence and adaptability, this was fairly radical for his time as many historians did not consider merchants worth mentioning in historical texts.

We don't know how Sima Qian died, he seemingly disappeared, but we do know he was castrated because of a preserved letter known as "Letter to Ren An" where he mentions this punishment. the reason he got castrated was because he defended the general Li Ling. in around 99 BCE Li Ling led a small force against a powerful nomadic group called the Xiongnu, while he fought hard he was severely outnumbered and after suffering huge losses Li Ling surrendered. This surrender made the Emperor at the time (Emperor Wu of Han) furious and he saw it as a disgrace. at court when officials started criticizing Li Ling, Sima Qian stood up in his defense arguing that he had fought bravely in a tough situation, the emperor saw this as an insult and sentenced Sima Qian to be castrated. interesting stuff really. we know a lot of this due to another historian Ban Gu. due to the "Letter to Ren An" we know quite a bit about how he felt. it's interesting to learn about Sima Qian as he was so modern for his time.
